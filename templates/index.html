{# --- motivation ------------------------------------------------------- #}

Why ILO Bank

Logic is learned by proving things. Proofs are hard to practise alone.

1

Too few exercises

A chapter ends with a dozen problems. Once they're done, there's nothing new to try before the contest.

→ New problems, each one checked by a proof checker before it's published.

2

No one to check your work

A proof on paper can look right and still misuse a rule, and you only find out when it's marked.

→ The editor checks every line the moment you add it.

3

No clear next step

It's hard to tell whether you're ready for quantifiers or still need the propositional rules.

→ A level ladder shows exactly what to work on next.

{# --- about ------------------------------------------------------------ #}

About

Built for students preparing for the ILO.

{# Who is behind the project, in their own words: edit this paragraph. #}

ILO Bank is built in the ILO Fellowship, to give students preparing for the International Logic Olympiad a place to practise the formal-proof side of the contest that is free, always open, and honest about mistakes.

It's an independent project, not an official Olympiad resource. The exercises follow the Fitch system of Stanford's Introduction to Logic, the same natural-deduction rules ILO students learn.

Proof editor
Chapters 5, 10, 13 and 15: propositional, quantifier, induction and equality rules
